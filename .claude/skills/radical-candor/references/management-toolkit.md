# Radical Candor — Management Toolkit

Deeper tools from Part II of *Radical Candor*, for ongoing management
situations rather than a single piece of feedback.

## 1:1 meetings

The 1:1 is the employee's meeting, not the manager's status update.

- Hold them regularly (weekly or biweekly), never cancel casually — a
  cancelled 1:1 says "you don't matter."
- The direct report sets the agenda. The manager's job is to listen and to
  ask questions that surface what's really going on.
- Good prompts: "What's on your mind?", "What's blocking you?", "What am I
  doing that's getting in your way?", "What are you not telling me?"
- Watch for warning signs: if 1:1s are all status and no substance, or the
  person never brings problems, trust is missing — go back to getting
  feedback and caring personally.

## The three career conversations

For understanding what motivates each person (do these over three separate
1:1-length conversations, in order):

1. **Life story.** "Tell me about your life, starting with kindergarten."
   Listen for what changes they made and why — the values driving them
   (e.g. they quit a stable job for a scrappy team → autonomy matters).
2. **Dreams.** "What do you want the pinnacle of your career to look like?"
   Get 3–5 different dreams, not one sanctioned "career path." Then identify
   the skills each dream requires.
3. **18-month plan.** "What do you need to learn or do in the next 18 months
   to move toward those dreams?" Translate into concrete actions, owners, and
   dates — most of them things the manager helps make happen.

## Rock stars and superstars

Two legitimate growth trajectories, both essential:

- **Rock stars** (steady growth) are a rock for the team: deeply skilled,
  happy in their current role, a source of stability and quality. Reward them
  with recognition and mastery — do NOT force them onto a promotion track
  they don't want, and don't treat lack of ambition-for-title as lack of
  ambition-for-craft.
- **Superstars** (steep growth) need constant new challenge and will leave if
  they don't get it. Give them the next hard thing, even knowing you'll lose
  them to their next step eventually.

Trajectory is per-person *and per-phase-of-life* — the same person can be a
superstar this year and a rock star next year (new baby, health, a passion
project outside work). Reassess; never file people permanently.

For persistent poor performance: if someone is doing bad work and candid
guidance plus support hasn't changed it, keeping them is unfair to them (no
honest shot at growth) and to the team (who carry the load). Part ways with
care: be direct about the decision, generous in the transition.

## Don't be the feedback middleman

When two people on a team complain to you about each other:

- Refuse to relay it. Insist they talk to each other directly first.
- If they can't resolve it, have them come to you **together** — never
  adjudicate based on one side's story.
- Escalating every conflict to the manager breeds politics; peer-to-peer
  candor is the goal state.

## The Get-Stuff-Done (GSD) wheel

For driving results collaboratively instead of by decree. The steps, in
order — skipping steps is what makes teams slow or resentful:

1. **Listen** — create space for ideas (quiet listening or loud "strong
   opinions, weakly held" listening — match your style, but make it safe).
2. **Clarify** — help ideas get sharpened before they're judged. Half-formed
   ideas die in debate; the boss's job is to help people make their case.
3. **Debate** — ideas fight, not people. Keep egos out ("it's not about
   being right, it's about getting it right"), and separate debate meetings
   from decision meetings.
4. **Decide** — push decisions to the people closest to the facts; the
   decider gathers facts firsthand rather than voting on summaries.
5. **Persuade** — the decider explains to everyone else using emotion, logic,
   AND credibility — "because I said so" doesn't scale.
6. **Execute** — minimize collaboration tax; don't waste the team's time on
   process that only serves the boss.
7. **Learn** — review results honestly, admit mistakes, and loop back to
   listening. Denial is the more common (and fatal) alternative.

## Applying this skill in coaching mode

When the user asks broad questions ("how do I build a better team culture",
"my team never tells me bad news"), don't dump the whole toolkit. Diagnose
where they are in the order of operations — usually the answer is earlier in
the chain than they think:

- Team won't speak up → they're not getting feedback *from* the boss safely
  yet → start with the user soliciting criticism and rewarding it.
- Feedback conversations go badly → check care-personally basics (1:1s,
  career conversations) before polishing feedback technique.
- Decisions feel slow or imposed → walk the GSD wheel and find the skipped
  step.
